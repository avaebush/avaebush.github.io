Earth system science for climate-informed resource governance

Earth Systems Science | Hazard Prevention | Data-Driven Solutions

Introduction

I am an Earth systems scientist investigating how climate variability and landscape evolution influence Earth surface processes, ecosystems, and natural hazards. My research combines geospatial analysis, statistical modeling, and community-engaged science to develop evidence-based strategies for climate adaptation and resource governance. As an educator, I support solutions-driven learning that connects science to environmental challenges through data analysis, quantitative reasoning, and collaborative inquiry.

Research

My research integrates artificial intelligence, climate science, and environmental data analysis to understand how Earth systems respond to natural and human-driven change. I combine quantitative analysis of environmental datasets with process-based understanding of landscapes, hydrology, and geomorphic systems. A central goal of my work is to connect research with experiential learning by engaging students with real environmental data, landscape processes, and evidence-based decision making.

Climate Variability, Land Use, & Landslide Hazard

My doctoral research investigated how precipitation variability, wildfire, and human modification of landscapes influence precipitation-triggered landslide hazard across the western United States. I integrated spatially distributed climate, wildfire, topographic, land-cover, and landslide datasets with quantitative statistical approaches to identify relationships among climate forcing, landscape conditions, and Earth-surface response. This work provides a foundation for applying data-driven and AI-enabled approaches to understanding environmental hazards under changing climate and land-use conditions.

Stream Sediment & Carbon Transport

Early research through the River Basins Research Initiative examined particulate organic carbon transport in urban headwater streams, including relationships among stream discharge, sediment load, and carbon dynamics. This work developed my interest in coupled hydrologic, sedimentary, and biogeochemical processes and in how material moves through river systems. It also established a broader research perspective focused on understanding environmental systems through the integration of field observations and quantitative environmental data.

Landscape Evolution & Earth Materials

My broader research experience spans geomorphology, geochemistry, tectonics, and environmental Earth science, with work involving landscape evolution, sedimentology, geological mapping, isotopic analysis, and characterization of Earth materials. These experiences provide a process-based foundation for investigating how sediment and other materials are produced, transformed, and transferred among connected landscapes. Together, they inform my current interest in linking Earth surface processes with environmental change, data-driven analysis, and emerging computational approaches.
